Collector Knowledge Base & FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Expert conservator guidance on caring for delicate Broderie Anglaise, laundering whitework linen, pressing embroidery without flattening stitches, and bespoke commissions.

What is the technical distinction between Broderie Anglaise and standard lace?

Standard bobbin or needle laces (such as Chantilly or Alençon) are constructed entirely from intertwining threads without any base fabric. Broderie Anglaise is a specialized cutwork embroidery executed onto a woven foundation cloth (such as cotton batiste or cambric), where geometric or floral holes are pierced and bound by dense needlework stitches.

How should a bespoke Broderie Anglaise garment be laundered?

We advise washing by hand in lukewarm distilled water using pure organic olive oil soap flakes or pH-neutral textile conservator detergent. Never wring, twist, or scrub the delicate eyelets. Gently press excess water out between two clean white terrycloth towels and air dry flat on a clean linen sheet away from direct sunlight.

How do you iron embroidered eyelet lace without crushing the raised stitches?

Never press an iron directly onto the face of the embroidery. Always place the garment face-down onto a thick, plush white towel or felted wool pressing mat. Iron on the reverse side of the fabric with gentle steam. The plush towel absorbs the raised satin stitches and cordonnet cords, keeping them plump and three-dimensional.

Why does authentic hand-pierced cutwork not fray along the hole margins?

In traditional hand embroidery, the holes are created with a smooth conical stiletto that pushes the warp and weft yarns aside rather than severing them with a blade. The embroiderer then binds these bunched, compressed fibers with tight buttonhole stitches, creating a reinforced circular perimeter that never frays.

Can antique heirloom whitework lace be safely restored at your atelier?

Yes. Our Mercer Street atelier maintains a dedicated conservation guild. We gently clean oxidized yellowing stains using biological enzymes, repair severed cutwork bridges using archival 100-count cotton threads, and re-anchor delicate scalloped picot edgings to return heirloom garments to pristine condition.

What is the production timeframe for a bespoke couture blouse?

A bespoke Broderie Anglaise blouse requires six to eight weeks from initial anatomical drafting to final delivery. This accommodates the hand-piercing and binding of hundreds of eyelets, two muslin prototype salon fittings, and eighty hours of meticulous hand needlework by our master artisans.

How should heirloom lace garments be stored to prevent yellowing?

Never store fine cotton or linen lace in plastic bags, which trap humidity and off-gas acidic polymers that cause irreversible brown spots. Store garments folded between sheets of unbuffered acid-free tissue paper inside breathable archival cotton garment boxes in a cool, dark, dry wardrobe.

What thread types are utilized for Broderie Flair commissions?

We source exclusively long-staple Egyptian Mako cotton thread (counts 50/2 to 100/2) and pure mulberry filament silk floss from French and Italian heritage thread mills. These natural fibers match the thermal expansion and shrinkage rate of our Swiss batiste, ensuring lifetime structural harmony.
